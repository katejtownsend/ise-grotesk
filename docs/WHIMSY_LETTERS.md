# The story letters

Ise Grotesk is built on a strict grid: one stroke weight, true circles, straight stems. A handful of letters carry one small detail each, borrowed from the buildings and moments in the lives of Walter and Ise Gropius. Gropius spoke of restoring "the magical element" beside the logical one. In this font the logic is the geometry, and the magic is the story.

Every story letter has a plain twin. Turn on the OpenType feature `ss01` ("Logic") to swap all of them for their plain forms.

## The letters

**I: the Fagus tower.** The Fagus Factory in Alfeld (1911 to 1913) is one of Walter Gropius's best-known buildings. The capital I has a slim shaft that tapers slightly toward the top, with a drum-shaped bulge about 40% of the way up, like the lettered drum on the factory's tower.

**W: the Fagus windows.** The factory's glass facade is a row of identical panes. Every point of the W, the four bottoms and the middle top, is a flat cut of exactly the same width, like a row of identical windows.

**i and w: Ise and Walter.** The lowercase i and w are their initials, and they repeat the Fagus details of the capitals: a soft drum bulge low on the i's stem, and five flat points on the w. They are kept mild, because lowercase letters need to stay easy to read.

**A: Apollo.** Ise edited and translated Walter's *Apollo in the Democracy*, a book about Apollo as a cultural symbol, with a chapter tied to their 1954 trip to Japan. A solid disc sits at the top of the A. It nods to Apollo and to the sun disc of the Japanese flag, and it makes the A read a little like a person with a head. The disc touches the cap-height line exactly, so line spacing is unaffected. On accented capitals (Á, Å and so on) the disc moves between the two stalks, to leave room for the accent.

**H: Hannover, 1923.** Walter and Ise met in Hannover in 1923. The crossbar of the H is split by a hairline gap in the middle: two halves that almost touch, like two people meeting. At small sizes the gap can close up, which is one reason Ise Grotesk is a display face.

**Z: the Isokon stairs.** From 1934 the Gropiuses lived in the Isokon Building (the Lawn Road Flats) in Belsize Park, London, designed by Wells Coates. Its stairs are a flight of steps between two decks, which is also the shape of a Z. The Z's diagonal is replaced by three steps, drawn at the same stroke weight as the decks.

**5: Ise's diaries.** Ise kept diaries from 1923 to 1928, five years of the early Bauhaus, written while she was known as "Frau Bauhaus". A small bookmark ribbon hangs from the top bar of the 5, like the ribbon marker in a journal. The figure's width is unchanged.

**&: curves and a straight line.** Gropius's 1923 exhibition was titled *Art and Technique: a New Unity*. The ampersand joins two kinds of form: curves (a circular loop above and a circular bowl below) and one straight diagonal that runs from the loop to the tail.

## Magic and logic

In his speech opening the Hochschule für Gestaltung in Ulm on 2 October 1955, Gropius argued that design should restore the magical element beside the logical one. Ise Grotesk follows that idea literally:

- **The default letters** are the "magic" set: a strict geometric skeleton, plus one story detail.
- **`ss01` (Logic)** swaps in the plain skeleton alone, for anyone who wants plain letters.
